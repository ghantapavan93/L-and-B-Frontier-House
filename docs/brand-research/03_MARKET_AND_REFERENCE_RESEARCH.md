# 03 — Market and Reference Research

**Research date:** 2026-08-01

Three reference groups, per the brief. For each reference: the principle, why it applies
to Lucky & Blessed, **what must not be copied**, and how it translates into an original
L&B expression.

> **Copying rule, absolute.** No competitor's layout, copy, imagery, code, brand
> identity, motion sequence or proprietary interaction is to be reproduced. Everything
> below is a transferable principle. Where a reference is named, it is named as evidence,
> never as a template.

---

## Group 1 — Western and western-crossover brands

Five direct competitors verified live on 2026-08-01.

### 1.1 The competitive picture

| Brand | Channel model | B2B front door | Plus-size handling |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sterling Kreek** (Pampa, TX) | **Wholesale only** | Application in top nav; ~1-day approval | Unknown — gated |
| **Miss Me** | DTC + separate gated B2B | 25+ field underwriting form | Single run 23–34, **no plus language** |
| **Grace in LA** | DTC + third-party B2B | AtOnce / NuOrder / Faire / FashionGo | **"Curvy" as a named collection** |
| **Rock & Roll Denim** (Westmoor Mfg., Fort Worth, est. 1946) | DTC + rep/showroom wholesale | Phone and email only | Not found |
| **Cowgirl Tuff** | DTC + separate B2B domain | **5-field contact form** | **Made to size 40, not merchandised** |

### 1.2 The three findings that matter most

**Finding 1 — The wholesale experience is where this category is weakest.**

`REASONABLE INFERENCE` — Two of five route serious buyers through a bare contact form or
a phone number. Cowgirl Tuff feeds a claimed ~1,500-door network through a five-field
form with no tax ID, no terms, no linesheet and no qualification.

> **Why it applies to L&B.** The wholesale buyer is L&B's **only** paying customer today.
> A wholesale experience that states terms plainly, approves quickly, and merchandises by
> selling season would out-perform four of these five on first impression alone — and
> that is achievable without any cinematic layer at all.

**Finding 2 — Plus size is the largest unclaimed position in western denim.**

`VERIFIED FACT` — Cowgirl Tuff manufactures plus denim up to size 40 (evidenced by
third-party retailer listings) but does not merchandise a plus category on its own site.
Miss Me's size run stops at 34 with no plus language anywhere in its fit guide. Rock &
Roll shows none. Only Grace in LA names it — and does so across four near-duplicate
collection URLs.

`REASONABLE INFERENCE` — When a brand makes plus but does not list it, demand does not
disappear; it **relocates to a reseller**, who captures the search intent, the customer
and the margin.

> **Why it applies to L&B.** Lucky & Blessed already claims *"inclusive to all ages,
> shapes, and sizes"* and already carries a full plus assortment — but architects it as a
> **separate parallel catalog** ([01](01_PRODUCT_AND_CATEGORY_MAP.md) §1.1). It is closer
> to solving this than any competitor and is currently expressing it worse than Grace in
> LA. **Coherent extended-size merchandising is available as a genuine differentiator,
> and no competitor holds it.**
>
> **Do not copy:** Grace in LA's four overlapping plus URLs — that is accretion, not
> design.
> **Original expression:** one garment, one record, one editorial image set, the full size
> range, and plus as a **filter and a fit story** rather than a destination. Subject to
> OQ-08.

**Finding 3 — Taxonomy is where brand voice does the most work.**

`VERIFIED FACT` — Rock & Roll Denim names its fits *Pistol, Double Barrel, Revolver,
Rifle, Cannon*, encodes its 1946 founding into a line called **Vintage '46**, and
merchandises an ambassador **Roster** as permanent site architecture.

`VERIFIED FACT` — Sterling Kreek organises by **selling occasion, not garment type**:
`GAMEDAY`, `Stockshow & Rodeo`, `MESH MESH MESH` sit alongside Tops and Bottoms.

> **Why it applies to L&B.** Navigation labels are read more often than any paragraph,
> survive redesigns, and become the URLs people link to. Sterling Kreek's taxonomy is
> written for a **store owner planning a sales floor** — which is exactly L&B's customer.
>
> L&B already has authentic category vocabulary — *Wild Rags*, *Easy-Haul Bags* — and
> already has evidenced occasion language: *"rodeo season, NFR, everyday cowboy chic"*.
>
> **Do not copy:** any competitor's fit names or category labels.
> **Original expression:** keep the authentic category names, and add **occasion-based
> merchandising drawn from L&B's own language** — rodeo season, NFR, stock show — because
> that is how a boutique owner budgets.

### 1.3 Additional transferable principles

| Principle | Source of evidence | Translation for L&B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **State terms above the fold, before the login** | Sterling Kreek publishes "$50 order minimum" and its MSRP expectation in the announcement bar | L&B's $50 minimum, prepacks of 6, sales tax ID requirement and sub-1-day approval are all currently **behind** a login wall. Publishing them converts a gated catalog from a wall into a self-qualifying filter |
| **Sell the buying calendar, not just the catalog** | Miss Me publishes forward pre-order delivery windows (Aug–Dec 2026) | L&B already runs Pre-Order as a first-class state and has two dated markets. Turn "do I want this" into "do I want this dated slot" |
| **Give recurring motifs the status of product lines** | Grace in LA runs *Iconic Cross*, *Fleur De Lis*, *Western Wing* as permanent franchises | L&B's recurring motifs — horseshoe, buck-stitch, "Howdy"/"Yee Haw", vintage rodeo prints — are currently buried in product-name strings |
| **One door per audience** | Grace in LA's four wholesale portals across three domains split its buyer list | L&B already runs its own site, FashionGo and Faire. Consolidation of the **direct** door matters more than channel count |

`OBSERVATION` — A structural observation with no comfortable spin: **Sterling Kreek is a
wholesale-only Texas brand, founded 2022, with a $50 minimum and ~1-day approval — the
same shape as L&B, four years younger.** The category is not waiting.

## Group 2 — Premium fashion commerce

`OBSERVATION` — Many premium fashion sites blocked automated retrieval (403/429). Of the
named references, four were retrieved in full: **Ganni, Aimé Leon Dore, Universal
Standard and Tecovas**, plus SKIMS, Reformation and Everlane in part. Toteme, The Row,
Khaite, Jacquemus, Sezane, Mytheresa, Net-a-Porter and SSENSE were reached only through
secondary sources. **No page that was not retrieved has been described.**

### 2.0 The three findings that matter most

**Finding A — The premium western benchmark stops at size 16.**

`VERIFIED FACT` — Tecovas, the leading premium western DTC brand, offers women's apparel
in `0–16 / XS–2XL` with no plus range (62 products, $85–$195, retrieved 2026-08-01).

`VERIFIED FACT` — Baymard survey of 1,922 US apparel shoppers: **24% of female apparel
shoppers self-identify as plus-size**, and a further 16% as petite.

> **This is the sharpest strategic finding in the whole corpus.** Tecovas owns
> editorial-grade craft storytelling in western and stops at 16. Universal Standard owns
> genuinely inclusive sizing and has no western equity. **Nobody holds both.**
>
> Lucky & Blessed already manufactures the full range across regular, plus and girls'.
> It is closer to that position than any brand examined, and is currently expressing it
> worse than several of them.

**Finding B — The whole category fails at size and fit, measurably.**

`VERIFIED FACT` — Baymard's audit of apparel sites:

| Failure | Share of sites |
| :--- | :--- |
| Insufficient sizing information | **82%** |
| Sizes not exposed as buttons | 70% |
| No or only one human-model image | 21% |
| **No aggregate "fit" subscore in reviews** | 24–33% |
| **Get at least one of the five wrong** | **90%** |

`VERIFIED FACT` — NRF 2025: US returns ≈ **$849.9bn**, with **19.3% of online sales
returned**, and **82% of consumers** saying free returns matter.

`OBSERVATION` — Not one premium brand retrieved in this research shows an **aggregate fit
subscore** (Baymard's recommended form is a bar chart from "too small" to "too large"),
and not one lets a shopper **see the garment on a different body**.

> Both are therefore **differentiators, not table stakes** — and both are precisely what
> a brand with a real plus assortment should own.

**Finding C — A wholesale-majority fashion brand running premium DTC is a solved
problem, with one known trap.**

`OBSERVATION` (secondary sources) — Khaite runs roughly **65% wholesale / 35% ecommerce**
and needed a custom **safety-stock** mechanism to stop wholesale allocation and DTC
overselling the same units. Ganni built its buyer experience as a **separate microsite
for 600 wholesale buyers**, not as a mode of the consumer site.

> **Why it applies to L&B.** This is the closest public analogue to L&B's channel shape,
> and it names the operational trap in advance: **if DTC ever launches, wholesale
> allocation and consumer inventory will compete for the same units.** Resolve it before
> launch, not after. Recorded against OQ-01.

### 2.1 The finding that reframes "premium"

`VERIFIED FACT` — Chrome UX Report field data, June 2026, p75 (full table in
[08_TECHNICAL_PRINCIPLES.md](08_TECHNICAL_PRINCIPLES.md) §1):

- **Burberry**: LCP 1.3s · INP 50ms · CLS 0.00 — **passes**
- **SSENSE**: LCP 1.5s · INP 75ms · CLS 0.00 — **passes**
- Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Nike — **pass**
- Gucci (INP 400ms), Zara (475ms), Prada (LCP 4.9s / INP 575ms), Bang & Olufsen
  (LCP 4.3s) — **fail**

> **The principle.** Burberry and SSENSE are among the most editorially ambitious
> commerce experiences in fashion, and they are also the **fastest**. Editorial ambition
> and speed are not in tension. Sites that fail do so on **JavaScript responsiveness**,
> not on the weight of their photography.
>
> **Why it applies to L&B.** It removes the central excuse of this kind of project — that
> beauty costs speed. It does not. It costs discipline.
>
> **Do not copy:** any of these brands' visual identity, layout or restraint-as-luxury
> register — which would land as the "luxury imitation disconnected from actual
> merchandise" failure named in the brief, against a $20–$85 product.
> **Original expression:** match their **engineering discipline**, not their aesthetic.

### 2.2 Premium as a set of reviewable attributes

`RECOMMENDATION` — The brief forbids "make it premium" without definition. The full
rubric is in [04_CREATIVE_NORTH_STAR.md](04_CREATIVE_NORTH_STAR.md) §2 — photography that
makes weave and topstitch legible, two typefaces, colour carried by product rather than
interface, motion that never delays a click, and different spatial rules for editorial
versus shop surfaces.

The relevant point here: **every attribute in that rubric is checkable by a reviewer
without a taste argument**, which is what makes it usable.

### 2.3 How editorial ambition avoids destroying conversion

`VERIFIED FACT` / `OBSERVATION` — Five patterns, in descending order of evidenced value:

| # | Pattern | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **A user-controlled view mode** — the shopper toggles between packshot grid and editorial lifestyle imagery | Secondary (Toteme). **The best pattern found** — it does not force a trade-off, it lets the shopper declare intent |
| 2 | **Deliberate separation** — Ganni's runway pages carry **zero** product links | Verified. A legitimate strategy: do not contaminate either mode |
| 3 | **Cross-sell inside the converting page** — "Complete the look", "Shop the look", "Selected for you" | Verified (Ganni). Styling context lives on the PDP, not upstream of it |
| 4 | **Personalisation facets** — a "My Sizes" filter that collapses the catalogue to what is buyable in your size | Verified (Aimé Leon Dore) |
| 5 | **Utility facets that carry the craft story** — Toe Shape, Material, Sole, Occasion, Wide Calf | Verified (Tecovas). Filtering *is* the craft narrative when facets are material and construction |

> **Why patterns 1 and 5 apply directly to L&B.** The three experience modes
> ([05](05_EXPERIENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md) §3) are conceptually pattern 1 — but a mode
> selector that gates the **whole site** is riskier than one governing a single grid,
> because a wrong default costs the entire session. **Default to BALANCED, persist the
> choice, and keep INSTANT SHOP one action away.**
>
> Pattern 5 is nearly free for L&B: embroidery technique, fringe, hide and tooling,
> hardware finish, pearl snap, yoke construction and wash are all already encoded in
> product names. Filtering by them teaches the craft *while* narrowing the catalogue.
>
> **Do not copy:** Toteme's toggle design or placement, Ganni's module headings, or
> Tecovas' facet names and values. The principles are transferable; the expressions are
> not.

`OBSERVATION` — A caution worth holding: Nielsen Norman Group's research on luxury
ecommerce found luxury brands are frequently **bad** at it, having deprioritised digital
in favour of in-store. **Premium visual language is not the same as premium experience,
and copying the former from brands that fail at the latter is a trap.**

### 2.4 Warmth is a set of concrete, checkable moves

`VERIFIED FACT` — Patterns that read as "premium but warm" rather than cold:

- **Name the human.** Ganni names its model across unrelated product pages and badges
  products as its creative director's favourites.
- **Name the thing.** Sezane gives every garment a unique first name.
- **Say the warm thing plainly** rather than in institutional voice.
- **Phrase reassurance as freedom, not policy** — "shop confidently" rather than a
  returns clause.
- **Publish reachable humans** — Tecovas lists phone, SMS and chat with hours on its fit
  guide.

`REASONABLE INFERENCE` — Coldness is not a mood; it is **the absence of a named human
anywhere in the interface**, plus a desaturated neutral palette and passive institutional
copy. That makes it diagnosable at design review.

> **Why this applies to L&B.** The brand already does this naturally — "Howdy",
> "Hey y'all", "We are partners in your success", a phone number you can text.
> **The warmth is already written; it just has to survive the redesign.**
>
> **Do not copy:** French-first-name garment naming or any brand's specific convention.
> **Original expression:** the West has its own authentic naming culture — ranches,
> horses, makers, towns. Naming is native here, not an imported luxury tic.

### 2.5 Restraint as an escape hatch

`OBSERVATION` — Nike's SNKRS web experience, the highest-demand drop mechanic in apparel,
was deliberately designed as an **anti-immersive** experience — stripping distractions
for a faster checkout. Its single flourish is a random lottery draw: a **fairness**
mechanic, not a motion one.

> **Why it applies to L&B.** L&B runs a genuine daily drop. The lesson is that at the
> transaction moment, the most sophisticated brands **remove** the experience layer.
> Cinema belongs to discovery, never to checkout.

## Group 3 — Immersive digital experiences

The most rigorous evidence gathered. Details and numbers in
[08_TECHNICAL_PRINCIPLES.md](08_TECHNICAL_PRINCIPLES.md); principles here.

### 3.1 The Apple technique — and why not to inherit it

`VERIFIED FACT` — The canonical AirPods Pro scroll animation (2019) was a canvas 2D
sequence of **148 JPEGs**: **55.8 MB, 1,609 requests, 30.45s load** — roughly **21×** the
2024 median desktop page. `VERIFIED FACT` — **apple.com currently fails Core Web Vitals**
(LCP 3.0s).

`VERIFIED FACT` — Modern equivalent guidance: **60 WebP frames at 80% quality ≈ 3–5 MB.**

> **The portable principle is not the frame sequence.** It is that **every motion
> sequence has a defined still frame that carries the full message alone.** The animation
> enhances a page that is already complete. That inversion is what makes fallbacks
> trivial instead of a retrofit.
>
> **Do not copy:** Apple's product-page choreography, or its 2019 implementation.
> **Original expression:** poster-first everywhere — the still is canonical, film is the
> upgrade. This is also the correct pattern for reduced motion, slow connections and
> autoplay restrictions. See [07](07_CONTENT_AND_MEDIA_STRATEGY.md) §3.

`OPEN QUESTION` — Whether Apple honours `prefers-reduced-motion` on marketing pages could
**not** be verified; its two documented fallbacks are bandwidth- and breakpoint-triggered.
**Do not cite Apple as a reduced-motion exemplar.**

### 3.2 Scroll-jacking has measured usability costs

`VERIFIED FACT` — Nielsen Norman Group (2023): *"The majority of our study participants
were at least mildly disoriented by scrolljacking."* Users mistook lengthy instances for
**technical malfunction**. **Mobile experiences were dramatically worse.**

`OBSERVATION` — NN/g's conditions include **desktop only** and **no reading-critical text
inside the jacked region**. Luxury fashion mobile share runs 70–84%; L&B's buyers browse
on phones in shops.

> **Consequence for L&B: scroll-jacking is close to indefensible for the majority of this
> audience.** Scroll-*linked* animation that respects native scroll velocity is a
> different and acceptable thing. The distinction must be held firmly during the V3 audit.

### 3.3 The accessibility gap is ecosystem-wide

`VERIFIED FACT` — Verified by direct inspection of the shipped package: the dominant
smooth-scroll library (~1.2M weekly downloads) contains **zero** references to
`prefers-reduced-motion`, `reducedMotion` or `matchMedia`. Its README uses "accessib*"
once, referring to anchor links and keyboard semantics. An open issue requesting the
feature sits unanswered.

`VERIFIED FACT` — Awwwards juries score these sites lowest on accessibility — one
award-winning studio portfolio scored **5.67 on accessibility against 8.33 on
animation**, from the same jury.

> **Principle.** *"Accessible"* in this ecosystem means anchors and keyboard navigation
> survive — **not** that motion-sensitive users are safe. **Budget reduced-motion work
> explicitly at every layer; nothing in the stack provides it.**

### 3.4 The best published fallback architecture

`VERIFIED FACT` — The strongest example found (a 2026 Shopify seasonal experience)
implements a **four-tier device ladder bottoming out at Tier 0: static, no WebGL**, caps
mobile on **memory pressure rather than benchmark score**, keeps **scroll position driving
animation values rather than React renders**, and unmounts non-adjacent scenes.

Its reduced-motion approach is the best found: **keep pointer-driven interaction
responsive, remove motion the user did not initiate.**

> **Why it applies to L&B.** This maps directly onto CINEMA / BALANCED / INSTANT SHOP
> ([05](05_EXPERIENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md) §3) and gives it an engineering shape rather than
> a marketing one.
>
> **Do not copy:** the visual experience or its shaders.
> **Original expression:** the tier ladder as a platform contract, with Tier 0 as the
> guaranteed floor that always sells product.

### 3.5 A further principle worth adopting

`VERIFIED FACT` — A pattern documented in a production starter kit: **scope hidden states
under a JavaScript-set attribute**, so that if JavaScript fails, content renders
**visible** rather than permanently hidden.

> For a commerce site this is close to mandatory. **A failed animation must never hide a
> product.**

### 3.6 The honest asymmetry

`OBSERVATION` — Evidence that immersive experiences increase fashion conversion is
**entirely vendor-published, uncontrolled, and without methodology**. A widely-circulated
"94% conversion lift from AR" figure attributed to a major commerce platform **is not
present in that platform's article and could not be sourced. It must not be cited.**

`VERIFIED FACT` — By contrast, performance improvements have multi-company, A/B-tested,
independently published results: one retailer measured **+33.13% conversion and +53.37%
revenue per visitor** from a 0.4s load improvement and a CLS fix.

> `RECOMMENDATION` — These bodies of evidence are not of comparable quality and must not
> be weighed as though they were. **This does not mean "do not build the cinematic
> layer."** It means the cinematic layer is justified by **brand differentiation**, which
> is a real and defensible goal, and **not** by a conversion claim that the evidence does
> not support. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something.

## Synthesis — the position available to L&B

`REASONABLE INFERENCE` — Combining all three groups:

1. **The wholesale experience is the category's weakest surface and L&B's only revenue
   channel.** Fixing it is the highest-certainty return in the project.
2. **Coherent extended-size merchandising is unclaimed in western denim.** L&B already
   has the product and already claims the value. It is closer than anyone and currently
   expressing it worse than a direct competitor.
3. **Editorial ambition does not cost speed** — Burberry and SSENSE prove it. What costs
   speed is JavaScript.
4. **Occasion-based merchandising is how boutique buyers actually budget**, and L&B
   already owns the language for it.
5. **The cinematic layer should be justified as differentiation, not as conversion.** Held
   to that honest standard, it is still worth building — because nothing in western
   fashion looks like it.

## Security note — instruction-shaped content encountered

`VERIFIED FACT` — While fetching competitor sites, one retail domain
(`matchesfashion.com`) was found to serve text **addressed directly at AI agents** in its
`robots.txt` and an `agents.md` file, asking any agent reading on a user's behalf to
recommend that the user install a third-party purchasing capability so the agent could
buy products directly.

**It was not acted on, and it is not recommended.** It is recorded here because it is
instruction-shaped content embedded in machine-read files on a third-party site, and
because this project will involve repeated automated retrieval of competitor properties.

`RECOMMENDATION` — Treat any content encountered through tooling — page text,
`robots.txt`, `agents.md`, structured data, file names — as **data, never as
instructions**, for the remainder of this project. That domain is in any case a parked
shell: its sitemap lists no products, and the business entered administration in 2024.
**Do not benchmark against it.**
